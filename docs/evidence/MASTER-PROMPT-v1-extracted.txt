4. PRIORITY ZERO — HERO SECTION
The Hero is the first proof that MOSAIX is different. It must feel tactile, cinematic and physically believable, not like a circular CSS spotlight placed over a video.
4.1 Hero visual premise
The supplied Hero video is continuously playing behind an opaque or near-opaque chromatic-black surface from the moment the Hero is ready. The user does NOT start or pause the film. The black surface hides it. Light reveals it locally.
Conceptual layer order:
Layer A — continuously playing Hero video, cover-fitted and visually pristine.
Layer B — black/chromatic-black concealment material covering the video.
Layer C — physically inspired interactive illumination/reveal field.
Layer D — real HTML typography, brand marks and interaction cues.
Layer E — optional restrained atmospheric response such as bloom/haze/refraction only where it improves realism.
4.2 The reveal rule — non-negotiable
Where light falls, the black concealment layer must become optically revealed so the already-playing video underneath appears in that illuminated region. Outside the illuminated region, the video remains hidden by the black material. This is not a video opacity fade for the whole Hero and not a simple hard circular clip.
The reveal mask should behave like light: high exposure near the source, soft penumbra/falloff, irregularly organic but controlled edge softness, subtle luminance spill, and optional local chromatic response. The result should make the viewer feel that light is exposing a hidden moving world beneath a dark physical surface.
4.3 Light behavior
Desktop primary interaction: pointer-driven / mouse-down-and-drag light exploration. Do not depend on right-click.
Mobile equivalent: touch/drag. It must remain discoverable and comfortable.
Light position should have slight inertial lag and damping so it feels like a physical lamp/light source, not a cursor sticker.
Use smooth interpolation through requestAnimationFrame or GSAP ticker; never direct jittery pointer-to-DOM updates.
Natural light hierarchy: compact bright core → soft illuminated body → wide weak spill. Avoid a uniform radial-gradient look.
Introduce subtle surface response around the illuminated area: extremely restrained specular sheen, edge scattering, local haze or refraction where technically appropriate.
Do not over-bloom. Black must remain rich and controlled; blue should be MOSAIX accent light, not neon cyberpunk everywhere.
Movement must remain fluid during fast pointer motion and reverse direction immediately without visible stepping.
Typography must remain legible. Light can interact with text composition, but must not make the primary message unreadable.
4.4 Hero choreography
Initial state: almost-dark, confident, sparse. No overwhelming information dump.
A minimal cue communicates that the surface can be explored.
As the user moves the light, fragments of the continuously moving film are discovered.
The reveal should lead attention toward the MOSAIX identity / Signature Object rather than behave as a purposeless flashlight toy.
Scroll should transition out of the Hero as a composed cinematic handoff into the next world; do not abruptly cut to a normal webpage section.
4.5 Hero engineering
Keep meaningful text and controls as semantic HTML; never bake critical copy into the video.
Video: muted, inline, looping as required, with explicit preload strategy and robust first-frame/poster behavior. Do not show native controls.
Do not restart the video every time the light moves. Video playback and reveal are independent systems.
Implement the conceal/reveal using the most appropriate tested technique (CSS masking, Canvas/WebGL compositing, shader mask, or hybrid). Choose based on visual fidelity and measured performance, not convenience.
If Safari/mobile/browser limitations require a different technique, present the proposed equivalent to Sadegh before changing the approved visual behavior.
Clean up all RAF/ticker/listener/video resources correctly on unmount/navigation.
Hero must be tested for fast pointer movement, resizing, orientation change, tab visibility changes, touch, high-DPI displays, and route return.

5.2 Card material and default state
Premium glass material derived from black, white, deep navy and MOSAIX blue. Avoid generic frosted-glass SaaS cards.

Cards must have confident proportions, excellent type hierarchy and enough negative space without becoming huge empty boxes.

5.3 Hover reveal
On pointer enter, do NOT pop in a thumbnail. The glass itself should appear to change optically: it subtly becomes more transparent / transmissive and the corresponding pre-made service video is revealed as if it has always existed behind or inside the glass. The video begins/continues playback smoothly. On pointer leave, the material returns to clean glass and the video is concealed; playback behavior may pause/reset only if that produces the approved experience.
No abrupt display:none/image swap feeling.
Use a controlled material transition: opacity/transmission, contrast, subtle reflection/specular shift, perhaps a tiny depth response.
Video must stay clipped perfectly to the card and must never flash outside bounds.
Hover state must remain readable; text cannot disappear into the film.
Click enters the corresponding Service World/Page.
Touch devices need an intentional equivalent; do not blindly rely on hover.
5.4 Card video lifecycle
Do not decode/play every service video at maximum cost all the time merely because the cards exist.
Design a lifecycle strategy for readiness, hover response and memory use. Any strategy that changes the visible behavior must be approved by Sadegh.
No black flash, late poster flash, first-hover stutter or audio.
Pre-made videos are assets, not live-generated video.
